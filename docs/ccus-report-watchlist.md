# CCUS institution and report watchlist

Updated: 2026-08-10 (Asia/Shanghai)

This is the recurring report channel for CCUS and energy-transition research. It complements the company watchlist; it does not replace project permits, government records, audited company disclosures or independent reporting.

The purpose is broader than tracking project announcements. Every CCUS research cycle must ask both:

1. **project state** — are individual projects moving from announcement to FID, financing, construction and operation?
2. **industry state** — are capital, costs, suppliers, transport/storage networks, policy and developer behavior forming a repeatable industry?

## Industry-state questions

Do not wait for a single project headline. Reports can create a material Signal when they change one of these long-horizon judgments:

### Capital and pipeline conversion

- How much annual investment is actually reaching CCUS, and which sectors/regions receive it?
- What share of announced capacity has reached FID, financial close, construction or operation?
- Are developers raising project debt and third-party capital, or remaining dependent on balance sheets and grants?
- Are cancellations and delays becoming more or less concentrated?

### Cost and technology learning

- Are capture, transport and storage costs falling on a comparable basis?
- Which cost components are moving: energy penalty, solvent/equipment, compression, wells, shipping, pipeline, monitoring or financing?
- Are repeat projects demonstrating learning, standardisation or modularisation, or is every project still bespoke?
- Are capture rates, uptime and energy performance matching design assumptions in operation?

### Supply chain and execution capacity

- What do FEED/EPC awards, equipment orders, vendor backlog and workforce studies say about future build capacity?
- Are long-lead compressors, absorbers, heat integration, wells, vessels, pipelines or monitoring systems constraining schedules?
- Are capable developers, EPC contractors and storage operators expanding or consolidating?

### Network-market formation

- How much transport/storage capacity is announced versus licensed, built, contracted and actually utilized?
- Are anchor customers and capacity-reservation structures producing bankable demand?
- Are tariffs and cross-border rules converging enough for third-party access and multi-user networks?
- Does storage appraisal and permitting progress fast enough to stay ahead of capture-project demand?

### Policy durability and regional divergence

- Which incentives are translating into FIDs rather than announcements?
- Are budgets, tax credits, CfDs, carbon prices and liability frameworks durable across political cycles?
- Why do project conversion, cost and network formation differ between North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific and the Middle East?
- Are public consultation, litigation or local acceptance becoming schedule constraints?

## Current report families to monitor

- IEA: **Financing CCUS at Scale (2026)**, the 2026 **CCUS Projects Database/Explorer**, CCUS business-model and storage handbooks, and CCUS-relevant Energy Technology Perspectives updates.
- Global CCS Institute: **Global Status of CCS** plus policy/legal/regulatory and technology reports.
- IEAGHG: continuously updated technical reports and GHGT proceedings covering capture, storage conformance, transport, monitoring and techno-economics.
- CCSA: Annual Review, Delivery Plan, workforce and supply-chain updates.
- ZEP: European CO2 market, infrastructure, tariff and transport/storage design work.
- OGCI: CCUS Hub Search and annual progress/business-model resources.
- Clean Energy Ministerial: global policy/programme inventories and implementation updates.
- ISO/TC 265: published/developing standards, especially geological storage, metering, ports, interfaces and emissions monitoring.
- DNV: CCS outlook and technical/risk publications.

The list is a discovery baseline, not a freshness claim. Each run must recheck the official publication page and record what changed since the preceding report or Signal.

## Report evidence contract

Every report candidate must record:

- report title, edition/version and verified publication date;
- the report's own data cutoff, not merely webpage update date;
- whether the complete report and methodology were reviewed;
- which statements are observed data, modeled estimates, scenarios or recommendations;
- geography, project-state definitions, capacity unit and time horizon;
- institution role and any membership, commercial or advocacy interest;
- concrete delta against the preceding report or existing Signal;
- which cross-cutting lens changed: capacity realism, economics/capital, bottleneck migration, demand/utilisation, regional divergence or falsification.

For pipeline reports, normalize at least these states before comparison:

```text
announced/planning → FID → financial close → construction → commissioning → operation
```

For transport/storage capacity, keep these separate:

```text
announced → licensed/permitted → built → contracted/reserved → injecting/utilized
```

If a data cutoff is not disclosed, record `not_disclosed`; the candidate must go to `needs_review`. Paywalled or members-only text cannot support an automatic Signal unless the complete source has actually been accessed.

## Promotion rules

- A report may establish what its authors measured, modeled or recommended. It does not by itself establish that a project is financed, permitted, under construction, commissioned or operating.
- A sector-level report can still be a strong Signal when the **industry-level finding itself** is the material evidence—for example pipeline conversion, investment, cost, supply-chain capacity or network utilization.
- Project counts must be normalized to the same project-state taxonomy before comparison.
- Announced, prospective and modeled capacity must never be added to operating capacity.
- Forecasts must retain base year, scenario, assumptions and range.
- Industry-association and coalition claims must be explicitly attributed and checked against government, regulator, project or independent records when they describe real-world delivery.
- A newer publication with unchanged underlying data is not a new Signal.
